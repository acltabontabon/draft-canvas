# Principles

## Your architecture, your agents, your decisions

Most AI diagramming tools ask you to describe a picture and then draw it for you. Draft Canvas works
the other way round: you reason about your architecture with an agent, on the canvas, and you decide
what changes.

- **Your architecture.** Diagrams are your files, on your device. Nothing is uploaded, and there is no
  account. What a diagram says about your system stays yours.
- **Your agents.** You bring the coding agent you already use, signed in as you. Draft Canvas gives it
  tools, not a model, a key or a subscription of its own.
- **Your decisions.** An agent explains, questions and proposes. You see every change on the canvas
  before it lands, and you accept it, reject it or ask for another way. Accepted changes are ordinary
  edits, undone like any other.

## What it means for new work

- An agent is a collaborator, not a command runner. A question gets reasoning, trade-offs and what the
  agent would need to know. Changes come when they are asked for.
- Changes arrive as proposals by default, drawn where they apply, with what they cover and what they
  leave open. Nothing an agent does skips review unless the person chose that.
- The canvas stays the subject. Agent surfaces point at the diagram; they don't take it over.
- No feature may need a Draft Canvas account, an API key held by Draft Canvas, or a server that sees
  diagrams.
- An agent is limited to what the person allowed, and Draft Canvas enforces that limit itself rather
  than trusting the agent's prompt to respect it.

See [Agent integration](agent-integration.md) and [Privacy](privacy.md) for how each of these is held
in place.
